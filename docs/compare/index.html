Mac dictation apps

Compared, including where we lose.

We make one of these, so read this with that in mind. What follows is the comparison we'd want if we were choosing: what actually runs on your Mac, what leaves it, what it costs, and which app to pick when it isn't ours.

Pick by what you care about

Nothing should leave my Mac
Plainsay Local · VoiceInk

Both offer local transcription without an account. Plainsay provides a free signed app; VoiceInk can be built free from source, while its official signed build is a one-time purchase. Apple's Dictation can also process on-device, depending on its settings.

I want the most polished product
Wispr Flow

It's the incumbent, and it shows in the details. The trade is explicit in their own docs: transcription always happens in the cloud, and it's $15/month.

I just want something now
Apple Dictation

Already installed and free. Check Keyboard settings: Apple says general text Dictation may be processed on-device, or sent to its servers, depending on the configuration. Apple's Dictation guide

When Plainsay is the wrong choice

Genuinely — these are the cases where we'd point you elsewhere.

You need Windows, Android, or an Intel Mac. Plainsay is Apple silicon only. Wispr Flow covers the most platforms. (A Windows port is in progress, but it isn't shipped.)

You want a first-class local cleanup integration. VoiceInk supports Ollama directly. Plainsay can keep Polishing local through a compatible custom endpoint, or leave it off, but it does not have a dedicated Ollama setup flow.

You have recordings to transcribe, not words to dictate. Plainsay has no file import at all — it cannot open a single audio or video file. MacWhisper was built for exactly that job and also dictates; we wrote a page on the difference.

You want to pay once and never think about it. VoiceInk's one-time purchase is a fair model and avoids depending on whether we keep the servers running.

Apple's Dictation already does what you need. It's free and built in. Its Keyboard settings indicate whether general text Dictation is processed on-device or sent to Apple's servers. We wrote a whole page on when it's genuinely enough.

What Plainsay is for

Local-mode dictation where recorded audio stays on your Mac, the Mac client code is readable, and there's no account or subscription in the way. Hold a key, speak, release — Plainsay attempts to insert the result in the active app and leaves it on the clipboard as a fallback.
