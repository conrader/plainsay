When Plainsay makes sense

If most of your writing happens on a Mac and you want to avoid recurring fees, Local mode is the starting point. Download a speech model once, choose a key, and dictate into the fields you already use. The MIT-licensed Mac client is available to inspect or build yourself. Cloud is never required to keep using Local.

When Wispr Flow may suit you better

Consider Flow when writing across several device types or choosing a service for a team. Its offering also includes meeting notes and team features; Plainsay focuses on dictating into text fields. Compare those broader needs alongside the price. Wispr’s plans and features

Offline speech and privacy

Plainsay Local can transcribe without an internet connection after model setup. Keep Polishing off or use a compatible local endpoint if transcript text should remain local too. Hosted transcription and hosted editing send data to the service you choose. For Flow’s current recovery behavior, read its linked documentation rather than assuming every platform behaves identically.

Questions before switching

Is Plainsay a free alternative to Wispr Flow?

Yes, for local dictation on an Apple silicon Mac. There is no subscription or weekly usage quota in Local mode. Advanced editing can require a configured provider; Cloud is optional.

Is Wispr Flow free too?

Yes. Its pricing page lists a free desktop allowance of 2,000 words per week. Pro removes the dictation limit. Check current plans.

Try it with your own words

Download Plainsay for an Apple silicon Mac running macOS 14 or later. Choose Local during setup, download a speech model, and dictate a few sentences in your normal apps. Review names, punctuation, and insertion behavior. No payment card or Cloud subscription is needed.
