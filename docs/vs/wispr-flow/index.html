Plainsay vs Wispr Flow

Same idea. One has a $0 mode that keeps your voice local.

In Plainsay Local mode, Whisper or Parakeet transcribes on your Mac — no account or subscription. Optional Plainsay Cloud offers hosted transcription. Wispr Flow is a cloud service with a limited free tier and a paid subscription; by its own documentation, transcription always happens off-device. Every claim about Wispr below links to Wispr's own pages.

Price

Wispr Flow has a limited free tier. Its paid plan is $15/month, or $12/month billed annually — $144 a year — with no lifetime or one-time purchase tier.

The current MIT-licensed Plainsay client runs locally for $0, with no word limits and no account. Every published MIT release remains free to fork and modify. If you'd rather not download a transcription model or manage a key, Plainsay Cloud is 12 PLN/month (about US$3), with up to 900 transcription minutes in any rolling 30-day window; Local mode is unmetered.

Your voice never has to leave the Mac

This is the difference that doesn't show up on a pricing page. Wispr Flow's own privacy documentation states that “transcription always happens in the cloud.” There is no offline mode — every word you dictate is uploaded, transcribed on someone else's servers, and returned.

In Plainsay Local mode, Whisper or NVIDIA Parakeet runs through Core ML on your Mac. Dictating needs no account, and recorded audio stays on the machine in your hands. Optional Plainsay Cloud and bring-your-own-provider transcription are separate modes that send audio to the selected service.

Honest about every place data can leave
Local-mode speech recognition keeps recorded audio on your Mac. Optional Plainsay Cloud and bring-your-own-provider transcription send audio to the chosen service. Optional Polishing can also send transcript text unless it is off or uses a compatible local endpoint. The honest claim is not “every mode is 100% offline”; it is that Local is the default, every speech and Polishing network path is a visible choice, and local dictation remains free. Wispr offers no local transcription mode.

Open, so you don't have to take our word for it

A closed-source cloud app asks you to trust a privacy policy. An open one lets you read the code.

Plainsay's entire Mac client is MIT licensed and public — the pipeline, the recorder, the settings, all of it. You can read exactly what happens to your audio, fork the client, and build your own copy. The optional Plainsay Cloud backend is a separate hosted service and is not open source. Wispr Flow is closed source — its answer to “what happens to my voice” is a privacy policy you're asked to trust. With Plainsay Local, the client-side answer is visible in the source code.

Where Wispr Flow is the better pick — honestly

  • You need Windows, iOS, or Android — Plainsay is macOS-only today.
  • You want a single cloud account that syncs across all your devices out of the box.
  • You'd rather never see a model download or an API key, and $144/year is fine for that convenience.

If instead you want a free Local mode whose source you can read and that keeps recorded audio on your Mac — that's Plainsay.
