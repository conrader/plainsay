When Apple Dictation is enough

If you occasionally dictate a short message, first try the feature already on your Mac. Apple documents how to enable it, choose its shortcut, and dictate into text fields. That may be all you need. Apple’s Dictation guide

When Plainsay makes sense

Plainsay is useful when you want to choose your speech model and keep dictation-specific vocabulary and History in one app. You can hold a key to speak or use tap-to-toggle recording. Start with free Local mode; add Polishing or a hosted service only when it helps your workflow.

Check processing settings in either app

Apple says Keyboard settings show whether Dictation runs on-device or sends audio and transcripts to its servers. Apple’s privacy notice In Plainsay, Local speech stays on your Mac. To keep transcript editing local as well, turn Polishing off or use a compatible local endpoint.

Questions before switching

Is Apple Dictation free?

Yes. It is a feature of macOS. Plainsay Local is also free, but requires installing the app and a speech model.

Do I need to replace Apple Dictation?

No. Try Plainsay in the text fields you use most and choose the workflow you prefer. Configure different shortcuts if you keep both enabled.

Try it with your own words

Download Plainsay for an Apple silicon Mac running macOS 14 or later. Choose Local during setup, download a speech model, and dictate a few sentences in your normal apps. Review names, punctuation, and insertion behavior. No payment card or Cloud subscription is needed.
