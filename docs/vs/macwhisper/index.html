Plainsay vs MacWhisper

Both run on your Mac. One is a suite, one is a shortcut.

This is not an on-device versus cloud comparison. MacWhisper transcribes locally too, and says so plainly. The real difference is what each one is for — and one of them cannot do the other's main job at all.

The short version

Both are genuinely on-device

MacWhisper states it uses "local models to transcribe your files" and lets you "process sensitive content locally without data ever leaving your Mac". Plainsay's Local mode does the same. If privacy is your reason for looking, both qualify.

MacWhisper does far more

It transcribes existing audio and video, produces subtitles, and handles batches. Plainsay does none of that — it has no way to open a file. It listens to a hotkey and pastes what you said.

Plainsay is free and readable

MacWhisper Pro is €64 for a licence with lifetime updates. Plainsay's Local mode is free, and the whole client is MIT-licensed on GitHub, so the privacy claim above can be checked rather than believed.

The part that is not a difference

Most comparisons on this site turn on where your audio goes. This one does not. MacWhisper is a local-first transcription app and has been since it launched; the claim that your recordings stay on your Mac is one it makes on its own homepage. We are not going to imply otherwise to make a point.

What we do offer that it does not is the ability to check. Plainsay's client is MIT-licensed and public, so "the audio never leaves your Mac" is a statement you can read the source for. MacWhisper's site does not state a licence, so the same claim there rests on trusting the developer — which many people reasonably do.

A suite versus a shortcut

MacWhisper began as a way to turn recordings into text: podcasts, interviews, lectures, video. Dictation came later, alongside subtitles, batches and meeting capture. It is a workbench.

Plainsay only ever does one thing. You hold a key, you speak, the text lands in whatever app you were already typing in. There is no library, no file list, no import. If you hand Plainsay a folder of recordings it cannot help you, and that is by design rather than by omission.

Where MacWhisper is the better pick — honestly

Buy MacWhisper if any of this is your actual work:

  • You have recordings to transcribe. Interviews, meetings, video, a backlog of voice memos. Plainsay cannot open a single one of them.
  • You need subtitles or timestamps. Not something a dictation shortcut produces at all.
  • You want one paid app that covers everything and would rather pay once than assemble tools.

Choose Plainsay when dictation is the job — when you want the words in the document you are already writing, for nothing, from a client you can read.
