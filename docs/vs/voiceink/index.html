Plainsay vs VoiceInk

Both can be free. The route is different.

VoiceInk is a real on-device dictation app for Mac: genuinely local transcription and GPL-3.0 source you can build for free. Its $29–69 purchase buys an official signed build and support, not access to otherwise closed code. Plainsay instead provides its signed and notarized MIT app as a free download.

The short version

Price
Two free paths

Plainsay's signed and notarized DMG is free, with no Local-mode word limits. VoiceInk can be built from its GPL source for free; its official signed build and support cost $29 (1 Mac) to $69 (3 Macs), once.

Transcription
Both offer local

Both can transcribe on your Mac. VoiceInk uses whisper.cpp, Parakeet, and other local engines; Plainsay Local uses WhisperKit or Parakeet via Core ML. Neither requires the cloud to dictate.

License
MIT vs GPL-3.0

Plainsay is MIT — permissive, no obligations on derivative work. VoiceInk is GPL-3.0 — also open source, but copyleft, and its repo currently isn't accepting outside pull requests.

Free download vs free self-build

VoiceInk is not a subscription trap, and it is not paywalled source. You can build the GPL-3.0 project yourself for $0. Its $29–69 one-time purchase is a fair charge for an official signed build and support. Plainsay removes the build step too: its signed and notarized Local app is a free download, while optional Cloud remains paid.

If you are comfortable building from source, both apps offer a genuinely free route. If you want a ready-to-run app, Plainsay is free; VoiceInk asks for a one-time purchase that supports its developer.

Openness: MIT vs GPL-3.0

Both are open source — this isn't a privacy argument the way it is against closed, cloud-only competitors. The real difference is the license terms and how open the project is to outside contribution right now.

Plainsay is MIT licensed: permissive, with no copyleft obligation on derivative work. VoiceInk is GPL-3.0: also genuinely open and free to self-build, but derivative distributions must follow its copyleft terms. As of this writing its GitHub repo isn't accepting external pull requests, while Plainsay welcomes them.

Where VoiceInk is the better pick — honestly

  • You want an iOS app too — Plainsay is Mac-only today, VoiceInk now supports iOS.
  • You are comfortable building from source and prefer VoiceInk's features — its GPL repository gives you a free route without buying the signed build.
  • You'd rather pay once for a supported, actively-maintained product than rely on volunteer open-source goodwill.
  • You want built-in support for a wider range of cloud cleanup providers out of the box (Groq, Cerebras, Mistral, OpenRouter, and more).

If instead you want a ready-to-run signed and notarized app at no charge, with a permissive MIT codebase open to contribution — that's Plainsay Local.
