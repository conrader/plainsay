2. The short version

  • Local transcription: recorded audio and transcript text stay on your Mac when Polishing is off or uses a local endpoint. No Plainsay account is needed.
  • Plainsay Cloud speech: the app sends recorded audio and limited request metadata to DMT. DMT processes it on an operated node when available or forwards it to deAPI as a fallback.
  • Hosted Polishing: transcript text and vocabulary terms go to the provider selected for Polishing. Plainsay Cloud routes them through OpenRouter to the configured model.
  • No advertising analytics: the Mac client has no analytics or telemetry SDK, and the website has no advertising or analytics cookies.
  • Content database: Plainsay Cloud does not store recorded audio or transcript text in its application database. It does store account, subscription, session, and usage metadata.

4. Data kept on your Mac

The following data is controlled through the app and is not uploaded to DMT merely because it exists:

  • up to 100 history records containing raw and final text, time, duration, insertion outcome, and the destination app’s bundle identifier; old records are overwritten and History can be cleared;
  • an interrupted recording in a private recovery folder until it is successfully processed or you remove it;
  • settings, languages, vocabulary, provider choices, and an optional 256-dimensional speaker embedding derived from the voice-enrolment sample;
  • provider API keys and the Plainsay Cloud session token in macOS Keychain; and
  • a temporary in-memory copy of clipboard contents while Plainsay attempts to paste and restore them.

History and pending-audio files use owner-only permissions and are excluded from normal backups by the app. The voice enrolment recording is converted locally to an embedding; the recording itself is not retained by Plainsay after enrolment. Deleting the app alone may not remove Application Support, model, preference, or Keychain data.

5. Data received by Plainsay Cloud

Account and authentication

We receive an email address when you request an email sign-in code. If Sign in with Apple is used, we receive Apple’s stable account identifier and, when Apple supplies it, an email address that may be a private relay address. We generate a random session token. Email codes are stored as salted hashes rather than readable codes.

Subscription and usage

We store an internal user ID, account creation time, Stripe customer and subscription identifiers, subscription status and period end, session creation and last-use times, and one usage record per operation. A usage record contains its time, type, audio duration, estimated upstream cost, and — for Polishing — input and output token counts. The application database deliberately contains neither recorded audio nor transcript text.

Speech and Polishing content

For Cloud speech, DMT receives AAC audio, clip duration, an optional language hint, and optional vocabulary terms. An available DMT-operated transcription node processes the audio first; otherwise it is sent to deAPI. On the DMT node, an upload is placed in an owner-only temporary directory for decoding, removed at the end of the request, and orphaned upload files are cleared when that service starts. Audio is not written to the Cloud application database.

Account information comes from you, or from Apple when you choose Apple sign-in. Subscription status and transaction identifiers come from Stripe. Request and usage metadata is generated when you use the service.

When Cloud Polishing is enabled, DMT receives the transcript and optional vocabulary terms and forwards them through OpenRouter to the configured Google Gemini endpoint. The service requires a zero-data-retention endpoint and denies provider data collection; if a compliant endpoint is unavailable, Polishing fails and the app retains the raw transcript instead.

8. Retention

  • Email sign-in codes: valid for 15 minutes; expired records are removed by an hourly cleanup, ordinarily within about 75 minutes of creation.
  • Cloud sessions: the service revokes the current session on sign-out and expires a token after 90 days without use.
  • Account, subscription, and usage metadata: kept while the Cloud account exists. After a verified deletion request, data not subject to an exception is removed within 30 days.
  • Payment, tax, accounting, complaint, and claims records: kept for the period required by applicable Polish or EU law, or until relevant claims expire. Stripe may keep its own records under its legal duties.
  • Website and update access logs: rotated daily and ordinarily retained for 14 days. API proxy access logging is disabled; minimal application event logs exclude headers, query strings, and request bodies and are removed under size-based system rotation.
  • Support correspondence: kept while the matter is open and thereafter only as needed for follow-up, legal duties, or claims.
  • Cloud content: not stored in the Plainsay application database. Provider-side transient processing and retention follow the controls and notices listed above. deAPI states that API content may be retained briefly for operational reliability; its exact contractual retention and distributed-processing safeguards remain a launch-review item.

A request for erasure does not necessarily remove data that must be retained by law, is needed to establish or defend claims, or belongs to a third party’s independent records. We will explain any applicable exception.

9. International transfers

DMT reviews provider roles, data-processing terms, subprocessors, transfer mechanisms, and retention for production routes. You may request information about the safeguard applicable to a particular transfer at hi@plainsay.app.

10. Your data-protection rights

Subject to the GDPR’s conditions and exceptions, you can request access and a copy, correction, erasure, restriction, portability, or object to processing based on legitimate interests. Where processing is based on consent, you can withdraw it without affecting earlier processing. You can also ask us to close a Cloud account; cancelling a subscription and deleting an account are separate actions.

Send a request to hi@plainsay.app or the postal address above. We may ask for proportionate information to verify that the request concerns your account. We normally respond within one month, with the extensions permitted by law for complex or numerous requests.

You may complain to the supervisory authority where you live or work or where the alleged infringement occurred. DMT’s competent Polish supervisory authority is the President of the Personal Data Protection Office (UODO), ul. Stanisława Moniuszki 1A, 00-014 Warsaw, Poland.

11. Sensitive data and other people

Dictation can contain health, political, religious, biometric, confidential, or other sensitive information, and a microphone can capture people nearby. Plainsay does not need those categories to run the service. Do not use a hosted mode for content you are not entitled to record or disclose. Local mode is the safer choice for confidential or special-category material.

If you dictate personal data about another person, you remain responsible for having a lawful basis to record and submit it. DMT processes that material only to provide the requested operation and does not use it for advertising or to build a user profile.

12. Website storage, cookies, and analytics

The public website sets no advertising or analytics cookies. Its language picker stores one value named plainsay-path in your browser’s local storage so the root page can return to the language you selected. The value stays until you clear site data or replace it with another language choice; it is not sent as a separate tracking identifier.

Stripe Checkout, Apple, GitHub, provider pages, and other websites you open from a link have their own storage and tracking practices. They are not loaded merely because you read this static page.

13. Security

Measures include TLS in transit, signed and notarized app distribution, signed update metadata, macOS Keychain for secrets, owner-only local recovery files, hashed email codes, random session tokens, Stripe webhook verification, a dedicated restricted server account, request-size and duration bounds, log redaction, and minimal Cloud database content. No system is perfectly secure. Report a suspected vulnerability through the private process in the project’s Security Policy, not a public issue.

14. Children

Plainsay Cloud is not directed to children under 16. A paid Cloud subscription may be created only by an adult or a person otherwise legally able and authorized to enter the contract. If you believe a child supplied account data without appropriate authorization, contact us so we can investigate and remove it where required.
