A few things
you might wonder.

Is it really free, or just a trial?

Local mode is free, with no subscription and no word or minute quotas. You don't need an account or a payment card. Cloud is optional—you can keep using Local without buying premium features.

Is Plainsay open source?

Yes. The Mac client is MIT-licensed. You can read the source, build it yourself, or contribute. Third-party models and components have their own licenses. The optional hosted Cloud service is separate and is not open source.

Can I use it completely offline?

Yes. Download a local speech model, then turn Polishing off or use a compatible local provider. Local speech recognition runs on your Mac. Cloud and hosted providers need an internet connection.

What leaves my Mac?

Local transcription keeps recorded audio on your Mac. Cloud sends audio for transcription and text for Polishing. Your own providers receive the data needed for the features you enable. The Mac client sends no analytics; automatic update checks can be disabled. Read the privacy policy.

Does it work in every app?

Plainsay works with many native Mac apps, browsers, and desktop tools. Some fields don't support reliable insertion or selection replacement. In those cases, copy the result and paste it yourself.

What does Cloud add?

Hosted speech recognition and Polishing, plus translation and email layout. You don't need to download a transcription model or configure provider keys. Cloud includes 900 transcription minutes in any rolling 30-day window. Local dictation remains unmetered.

Is Voice Edit included with Cloud?

Simple last-dictation replace and undo commands work locally with any speech service. Broader AI rewrites and selected-text Voice Edit currently need Gemini, Anthropic, OpenAI, OpenRouter, or a compatible custom provider configured in Polishing. A Cloud subscription alone does not enable those rewrites.
