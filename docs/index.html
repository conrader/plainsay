The cleanup pass removes fillers and false starts, fixes punctuation, and keeps your actual wording — it is not a rewrite. Provider is swappable (Gemini, OpenRouter, OpenAI, Claude, or a custom endpoint), it can be switched off entirely, and any failure falls back to the raw transcript instead of failing the dictation.

Why Plainsay

Open source, transparent, safe

Trust isn't a claim here — it's something you can go read. The whole client is MIT licensed and public: the pipeline, the HUD, the settings, all of it. Not open core, and not "open now, closed later" — free to fork, modify, and self-host indefinitely.

WHAT'S OPEN

The entire dictation client — transcription, editing, the HUD, Settings, the Setup Assistant. On-device, Plainsay makes no network call you didn't ask for: no analytics, no telemetry, no phoning home.

WHAT ISN'T

The billing backend for the paid Cloud tier — it holds payment and provider credentials, which is exactly why it stays separate and private. The dictation experience you actually use is fully open.

Three ways to run it

The free local tier is the default recommendation, not a funnel. Cloud exists for people who want zero setup — not because local is hobbled.

Reliability

A dictation is never silently lost.

Editing fails — no key, no network, a timeout

Falls back to the raw transcript automatically, with a HUD notice.

Nothing was focused to paste into

Text stays on the clipboard, with a HUD prompt to press ⌘V.

Crash or force-quit mid-recording

Audio is staged to disk before transcription, and recovered on next launch.

A paste fails for any other reason

The dictation is already in local history — written there before insertion was attempted.
