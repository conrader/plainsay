01 — How it works

Three phases, no menus in the middle.

The whole interaction is the hotkey. Everything else — model, provider, cleanup — is a choice you make once in Settings, not every time you dictate.

LISTENING

Hold, or tap once

Hold your hotkey and speak — release when you're done. Or tap once to latch recording on and tap again to stop, if holding a key while talking isn't comfortable.

TRANSCRIBING

Whisper or Parakeet, on this Mac

OpenAI Whisper via WhisperKit, or NVIDIA's multilingual Parakeet TDT via FluidAudio — both run through Core ML on Apple silicon. Or skip the download entirely with Plainsay Cloud or your own API key.

POLISHING

Filler words out, your wording in

An editing pass removes "um"s and false starts and fixes punctuation — without rewriting what you actually said. Turn it off entirely and you get the raw transcript instead.

02 — Reliability

A dictation is never quietly lost.

Every failure mode in the pipeline has a fallback that keeps your words, even when something downstream doesn't cooperate.

→ EDITING FAILS

Falls back to raw

No key, no network, a timeout, an HTTP error — you still get the unedited transcript, and the HUD says it fell back. Editing is best-effort by design.

→ PASTE HAS NOWHERE TO LAND

Stays on your clipboard

If nothing was focused to paste into, Plainsay leaves the text on the clipboard instead of silently discarding it — and says so.

→ THE APP QUITS MID-RECORDING

Recovered on next launch

Audio is written to disk before transcription starts. A crash or force-quit mid-dictation gets picked back up and transcribed the next time Plainsay opens.

→ A WORD GETS MISHEARD

You teach it

Add names and jargon to your vocabulary once. Whisper and hosted models use them as decoder hints; every model uses them again during editing.

03 — Choose how it runs

Local, hosted, or your own key.

Every provider — transcription and editing both — is a swappable protocol underneath, not a hard-coded assumption. Pick based on what you're optimizing for.
